## Transcription and units

The p. 392 table gives the following one-direction displacements, in fringe widths, after Fizeau reduced them to a common bulk-flow speed of 7.059 m/s:

`0.200, 0.220, 0.240, 0.167, 0.171, 0.225, 0.247, 0.225, 0.214, 0.230, 0.224, 0.247, 0.224, 0.307, 0.307, 0.256, 0.240, 0.240, 0.189`.

Their sum is the printed 4.373. Their arithmetic mean is 0.2301578947..., agreeing with the printed 0.23016 after rounding. The source's accompanying differences are differences from 0.230, not measurement uncertainties. They are not error bars.

Fizeau states that most observations used 7.059 m/s, some 5.515 m/s and some 3.7 m/s, but does not map those original speeds to individual rows. The repeated speed column is therefore explicitly a **reduction basis**, not nineteen independent speed measurements. The source's “double displacement” is twice the one-direction mean, approximately 0.46; it is not a second independent dataset.

The calculation on p. 394 uses tube length 1.4875 m and two tube traversals per ray, giving 2.975 m of moving water per beam. The earlier apparatus description on p. 388 rounds the length to 1.487 m. The preset uses the calculation's explicit length. Page 395 selects the E line of the solar spectrum at 0.000526 mm, converted to 5.26e-7 m. This is Fizeau's selected wavelength for the calculation, not a claim that the illumination was monochromatic.

The live comparison uses the existing shelf-optics reference owner, modern SI c, and an explicitly assumed refractive index of 1.333. The source pages used here do not print a numerical refractive index or light speed. No values are inferred backward from the printed predictions. Consequently the live numbers are a modern recalculation under stated assumptions, not an exact reconstruction of Fizeau's printed 0.4597 and 0.2022. Those printed predictions remain separately attributed text.

## Precision, dependencies and limits

- The last printed place of each displacement is 0.001 fringe. Half a printed place is 0.0005 fringe, a transcription rounding allowance only. No experimental standard error or confidence interval is supplied by this table.
- All rows share the apparatus, the bulk-flow reduction and the selected optical model. No independent-trial statistical fit or confidence claim is admitted.
- On pp. 398-399 Fizeau says dividing discharged volume per second by tube area gives the bulk mean velocity, whereas the rays cross the central region where water moves faster. He could not measure the necessary correction. His illustrative 10%, 15% and 20% adjustments are not independent measurements. They are not applied or fitted here.
- Changing a live control changes a prediction, never the transcribed rows. A flow-reversal calculation is distinguished from the table's one-direction protocol; the comparison does not silently double or sign-change the observations to manufacture agreement.
- Agreement at first order does not distinguish Fresnel's ether account from the later relativistic velocity-addition account. The latter remains labeled later and is not a 1904 premise.
