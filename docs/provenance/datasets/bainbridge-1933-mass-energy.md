## Transcription and scientific meaning

The numerical comparison is printed in prose, not a data table. Each row below is a different
reaction discussed in the letter; the rows are not repeated observations of one process.

| Reaction | Reported energy gain (MeV) | Printed mass equivalent | Independent mass difference | Quoted mass error |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Lithium-7 + proton → two alpha particles | 16.97 | 0.0182 | 0.0181 | ±0.0006 |
| Lithium-6 + deuteron → two alpha particles | 23.7 | 0.0255 | 0.0238 | ±0.0004 |

Lowered final digits in the printing remain decimal digits: `16.9₇`, `0.018₁`, `0.025₅`, and
`0.023₈`. They are not subscripts identifying distinct quantities. MeV is only a notation for
the printed million electron-volts; the stored values are not a conversion using today's
elementary charge.

All mass figures remain on the explicitly printed **oxygen-16 scale**, where oxygen-16 has
mass 16. The record's unit `O16-mass-unit` denotes that historical physical scale. It is not
the modern carbon-12 unified atomic mass unit. No conversion to modern `u` or kilograms is
performed, and the independent masses are not reconstructed using energy equivalence.

For the first reaction, Bainbridge draws the energy estimate from Cockcroft and Walton's
alpha-particle observations. The mass difference uses Aston's helium and hydrogen masses
and Bainbridge's lithium-7 value. The letter says the agreement is within the probable error.

For the second, the interpretation assumes lithium-6 captures deuterium and produces two
alpha particles. Its energy estimate uses momentum conservation and an extrapolation of
alpha-particle range–energy data. The mass comparison uses Aston's helium value and
Bainbridge's deuterium and lithium-6 values. The letter finds this comparison unsatisfactory,
then says stricter comparison needs the error in determining the alpha-particle ranges.

The letter gives **no numerical error for either reported energy gain**. Both cells therefore
carry `kind: missing`. The quoted mass errors are preserved without calling them standard
deviations, confidence intervals or a complete uncertainty budget. The comparison owner
subtracts the printed mass equivalent from the independently measured mass difference,
and divides the absolute difference by the quoted mass error only as a descriptive scale.
The resulting ratios, about 0.167 and 4.25, have no assigned probability or significance level.

The source reports a positive mass defect, reactants minus products. That convention is
separate from ME-03's signed change of mass inside a chosen system boundary. No historical
mass figure is passed off as an output of the interactive 1905 boundary ledger.

## Dates and limitations

The issue heading gives 15 July 1933; the letter is dated 16 June 1933. Neither is an observation
date. The energy and mass figures combine several separately cited investigations whose
measurement dates are not stated here. The series descriptions explicitly retain that absence;
no publication date is copied into an observation-date field.

Only the reported numerical facts and their source roles are transcribed. No particle-level
observations, uncertainty distribution, covariance, calibrated nuclear model or pooled fit is
invented. Both the agreement and the unresolved discrepancy remain visible.
