# Comprehension Facilitator Script & Protocol

## Phase 1: Pre-Briefing & Stance (5 minutes)

### Facilitator Welcome & Setup
*"Thank you for meeting with us today. My name is [Facilitator Name], and I am facilitating this session for the Annus Mirabilis edition project.*

*Before we begin, the most important thing to know is that **we are testing our website and explanations, not you**. You cannot make a mistake or give a wrong answer today. If anything feels confusing, unclear, or frustrating, that is an issue with our design and writing, and finding those points is exactly why we run these sessions.*

*We will spend about 30 to 45 minutes together. You can take a break whenever you like, and you can stop the session at any time without giving a reason. Stopping early will not affect your compensation in any way.*

*Do you have any questions before we look at the consent form?"*

---

## Phase 2: Accomplishment Selection & Baseline (5 minutes)

### Determining Chosen Activity
*"When you explore a physics paper like this, what are you most interested in doing today? For example, you might want to:*
- *Appreciate why the historical problem mattered;*
- *Explain the main physical idea in your own words;*
- *Predict what happens when numbers or conditions change;*
- *Derive the mathematical formulas step-by-step; or*
- *Critique the assumptions, evidence, and limitations.*

*There is no preferred choice; whatever you want to focus on is what we will follow."*

*(Record initial accomplishment on Observation Sheet).*

### Baseline Pre-Question
*"Before we open the interactive passage, here is a quick scenario: [Read paper pre-question from question bank]. In your own words, what comes to mind when you think about that situation?"*

*(Record baseline thinking without giving feedback or indicating correctness).*

---

## Phase 3: Interactive Exploration & Think-Aloud (20 minutes)

### Instructions
*"Now, please open the link on your screen. As you read, scroll, or interact with the sliders and buttons, please 'think aloud'—tell me what you are looking at, what makes sense, and what surprises you."*

### Neutral Think-Aloud Prompts
When the participant pauses or appears contemplative, use ONLY neutral, non-leading prompts:
- *"What are you noticing right now?"*
- *"What are you looking for here?"*
- *"What do you expect would happen if you changed this slider?"*
- *"Where would you look to find out why this step holds?"*
- *"What does that label mean to you?"*

### Strictly Forbidden Facilitator Actions:
- DO NOT say "Good job!", "Right", or "Correct".
- DO NOT say "Notice how equation 3 implies..." (leading).
- DO NOT rush the participant past an obstacle.
- DO NOT defend the design or explain how it was intended to work.

### Logging Stumbling Points
When an obstacle occurs, record:
- Standard Code (`undefined-symbol`, `omitted-inference`, `misleading-visual-cue`, `inaccessible-control`, `too-much-at-once`)
- Target Anchor / Action ID (`#s4-formula-2`, `bm-01:step-drag`)
- Specific verbatim quotation or observed behavior.

---

## Phase 4: Post-Explanation & Transfer Case (10 minutes)

### Core Reconstruction
*"Now that you have explored this section, how would you explain the main argument to someone who has never seen it before?"*

### Transfer Case
*"Let's look at a slightly different situation: [Read transfer question from question bank]. How would the reasoning you just saw apply here?"*

*(Evaluate against Meaning, Mechanism, Prediction, Assumptions, Evidence rubric dimensions).*

---

## Phase 5: Debriefing & Distress Procedure (5 minutes)

### Debriefing Questions
1. *"What was the clearest part of this lesson?"*
2. *"Where did you feel like you had to make a leap without enough explanation?"*
3. *"If you could change one thing about the diagram or text, what would it be?"*

### Distress / Fatigue Procedure
If at any point the participant displays signs of cognitive fatigue, frustration, or discomfort:
1. Immediately pause the task.
2. Say: *"Let's take a quick pause here. Remember, you have worked hard and this material is challenging. Would you like to take a 5-minute break, or would you prefer to stop here for today?"*
3. Respect the participant's decision immediately without pressure.
