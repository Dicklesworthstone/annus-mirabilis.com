## Begin with what the accounts determine {#unknown-absolute-energies}

Knowing a change does not require knowing an absolute total. An account can say how much energy a body gives away while leaving its internal energy before and after entirely unspecified. The mass–energy paper makes use of that distinction by comparing accounts of the same emission in different inertial frames. {#a-change-without-a-total}

The [source describes a body at rest emitting equal amounts of light in opposite directions](ref:mass-energy/s0-p7). Their symmetry removes recoil in the body's rest frame. Call their total energy in that frame L. A second observer moves at a fixed speed relative to the body, and writes a second account of the same event. Nothing here assigns the body an absolute energy from a presumed relation between energy and mass. {#same-emission}

The [light-energy transformation is imported from the relativity paper](ref:mass-energy/arg-me-import). For each pulse it depends on the frame speed and the angle between the pulse and the motion. This is an input to the argument. Conservation alone does not tell the moving observer how much energy the light carries. {#an-admitted-import}

Using the same C in both relations is the premise that permits it to cancel. The subtraction neither measures C nor proves that it remains the same. With that premise, the difference of frame-energy differences becomes the kinetic-energy decrease. {#cancel-the-common-constant}

If the offset could change, a change in C would remain beside the kinetic-energy change. Conservation would then determine their sum, not either part separately. In [the instrument](ref:me-01), relax the unchanged-offset premise after inspecting the balances. The admitted information no longer fixes the kinetic-energy change even though the light-energy accounts still have values. Losing that inference is the consequence of removing information, not a numerical failure. {#remove-the-premise}

This distinction is also the place to keep the historical disagreement visible. The edition's historical note records that H. E. Ives criticised the 1905 derivation in 1952, while John Stachel and Roberto Torretti defended its logical soundness in 1982. Their cited abstracts support those opposed assessments; they do not supply a verdict here on the full debate. The source premise can be stated and tested as a condition without pretending that the cancellation settles that disagreement. {#historical-disagreement}

## Find the coefficient at low speed {#compare-the-kinetic-coefficient}

The body has the same velocity in the moving frame before and after this symmetric emission, but its kinetic energy has decreased. To identify a change in inertia, the paper compares the leading small-speed term with the Newtonian kinetic-energy expression. The [source explicitly discards fourth and higher orders in speed](ref:mass-energy/s0-p11) at this step. {#where-the-approximation-enters}

The coefficient multiplying one half of the speed squared is L divided by c squared. This identifies the mass decrease within the argument's premises. It does not make the quadratic approximation exact at finite speed, and it does not determine the body's absolute rest energy. [The coefficient instrument](ref:me-02) keeps the exact difference, the approximation and the limiting coefficient distinct. {#coefficient-not-finite-speed-proxy}

If the step from a change in kinetic energy to its mass coefficient is unfamiliar, open [the work-and-energy foundation](ref:work-energy), then return to these balances. The reason the unknown internal energies do not obstruct the comparison is that the argument needs a particular change, not their absolute values. {#return-from-the-foundation}

## Ask what makes a nuisance term common {#apply-the-cancellation}

The transferable question is specific: which unknown quantity appears in both accounts in the same way? Write it down before cancelling it, and state what would happen if it changed. A successful cancellation is informative because the common-term premise is visible and can be challenged. It is not a general permission to remove inconvenient unknowns from a model. {#a-checkable-common-term}
